Task 01
The Downsides of Being Clever阅读材料: David Robson, The surprising downsides of being clever, BBC Future, 2015 年 4 月 14 日.

词汇释义
Explain the following using other words in English

Task 01 · Vocabulary

Explain the following using other words in English:

  1. ramifications
  2. new-fangled
  3. to be a ball and chain
  4. agonising over the human condition
  5. ruminate over something
  6. unbiased decision-making
  7. to be a rounded argument
  8. to admit one's own foibles

翻译: 用别的英文说法解释下面这些词和短语.

思路: 先回到文章里找到它出现的那一句, 按上下文理解, 再换成更普通的词说一遍. 三条要求: 词性不变 (动词还解释成动词短语, 形容词还解释成形容词); 不要用原词本身或它的同根词 (解释 unbiased 时不要写 not biased); 写完放回原句读一遍, 意思通顺就对了. 习语 (ball and chain) 先想字面画面, 再说它的比喻义.

答案 (先自己做再看)
#表达换个说法文中的用法
1ramificationsknock-on effects; consequences that spread further than you'd expect研究这些天才儿童的结论, 影响的不只是少数聪明人, 而是整个教育和智力测试体系.
2new-fanglednew and not yet trusted; a bit of a novelty1920 年代的 IQ 测试刚出现不久, 还算个新鲜玩意儿. 这个词常带一点不以为然的口气.
3to be a ball and chainto be a burden that holds you back字面是绑在囚犯脚上的铁球和铁链. 文中指「知道自己有天赋」反而成了包袱: 老了回头看, 总觉得没达到年轻时的期望.
4agonising over the human conditionworrying yourself sick about the big questions of life and why people are the way they are大家想象中聪明人晚上睡不着, 在为人生和人性发愁. 文章随后说: 他们确实更焦虑, 但担心的多是日常琐事.
5ruminate over somethingto keep going over something in your mind本义是牛反刍. 文中说语言能力强的人更容易把烦恼说出来, 也更容易翻来覆去地想.
6unbiased decision-makingmaking choices fairly, based on the evidence rather than on what you already believeStanovich 发现, 做决定公不公正, 和 IQ 高低基本没有关系.
7to be a rounded argumentan argument that looks at the issue from several sides and doesn't leave out the awkward detailsGrossmann 的实验里, 专家评判受试者的论证全不全面, 是评「智慧」的标准之一.
8to admit one's own foiblesto own up to your small weaknesses and quirks文章最后说, 最难的是让聪明人承认自己的小毛病. foible 指无伤大雅的小缺点, 比 flaw 轻.

易错: ① ramifications 几乎总用复数, 指一连串后果, 不是单个的结果 (result). ② rounded 在这里不是「圆的」, 是「全面的、面面俱到的」, 同 well-rounded. ③ foibles 不要解释成 mistakes: 它是性格上的小弱点, 不是做错的事.

一句话概括全文
Write the gist of the article in one sentence

Task 01 · Gist

Write the gist of the article in one sentence.

翻译: 用一句话写出这篇文章的主旨.

思路: gist 是主旨, 不是摘要: 只写作者最终想说的那一个观点, 例子、人名、实验细节一概不要. 这篇文章的结构是「常见看法 → 证据推翻它 → 真正起作用的是什么」, 一句话里最好把这三步都带到: 聪明并不等于更幸福或更理性, 真正有用的是另一种东西.

答案 (先自己做再看)

A high IQ doesn't make you happier or more rational, since clever people worry more and are just as biased as anyone else; what actually leads to good judgement is wisdom, and wisdom can be trained.

更短的版本: Being clever won't make you wise or happy, but learning to judge fairly might.

前一句对应文章的三步: 前半句是 Terman 的「白蚁」 (Termites) 研究和焦虑研究, 中间是 Stanovich 的偏见研究, 后半句是 Grossmann 的「智慧」研究. 两个版本都没有提任何人名或数字, 这正是 gist 和 summary 的区别.

易错: 只写「聪明人不快乐」是不够的, 那只是文章的前一半. 作者的落点在后面: 判断力和生活质量取决于智慧, 而智慧可以练.

文章脉络 (方便复习时快速回忆):

  • 开头: 人们常觉得天才都孤独、焦虑. 问题是: 高智商到底是福还是累?
  • Terman 的「白蚁」 (Termites): 1920 年代起追踪 1500 名 IQ 140 以上的儿童. 结果: 智力和成就并不完全相关, 离婚、酗酒、自杀率和普通人差不多; 不少人晚年觉得辜负了年轻时的期望.
  • 焦虑: Penney 发现高 IQ (尤其语言智力高) 的学生更常担心, 但担心的多是日常小事, 不是人生大问题.
  • 偏见: Stanovich 发现理性决策和 IQ 基本无关; 聪明人同样有「我方偏见」, 甚至更看不到自己的偏见, 也会犯赌徒谬误.
  • 智慧: Grossmann 用社会难题测「智慧推理」 (论证是否全面、是否承认自己知识有限). 得分高的人生活满意度更高、更少焦虑, 而这和 IQ 无关. 智慧可以训练, 比如用第三人称谈自己的问题.
  • 结尾: 难的是让聪明人承认自己的小毛病. 苏格拉底: 最聪明的人也许是承认自己一无所知的人.

Task 02
Women Presidents on Screen阅读材料: Nicholas Barber, In 1924 a risqué silent film featured a woman as US president - what happened on screen in the 100 years that followed?, BBC Culture, 2024 年 10 月 23 日.

热身: 女性政治领袖
Warm-up: Female political leaders

Task 02 · Warm-up
  1. Name as many past and current female political leaders as you can:
  2. Are there any similar characteristics which could link these leaders?
  3. Do you think it is generally more difficult for a woman to be an effective leader? Why?/Why not?

翻译: 1. 尽可能多地列出过去和现在的女性政治领袖. 2. 这些领袖有没有共同的特点? 3. 你觉得女性总体上更难成为有效的领导者吗? 为什么?

思路: 第 1 题只要名字, 不同国家都列一些. 第 2 题从出身 (政治家族)、上台的方式 (危机中接手)、外界怎么评价她们这几个角度找共同点. 第 3 题是观点题, 先给明确立场, 再用一两个理由撑住; 把「能力」和「别人怎么看她」分开说, 论证会清楚很多.

答案 (先自己做再看)

1. Angela Merkel, Margaret Thatcher, Theresa May, Indira Gandhi, Golda Meir, Benazir Bhutto, Jacinda Ardern, Julia Gillard, Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, Michelle Bachelet, Tsai Ing-wen, Sanna Marin, Giorgia Meloni, Claudia Sheinbaum, Ursula von der Leyen.

2. Many of them came from political families. Indira Gandhi and Benazir Bhutto are the obvious examples. Others worked their way up through party politics for years before they got the top job. A lot of them were seen as tough or very pragmatic, and several came to power during a crisis. Most were also judged on their looks and personal lives far more than male leaders.

3. Not really. A woman can lead just as well as a man, but she usually has a harder time getting there and being taken seriously. If she's firm, people call her cold. If she's warm, people call her weak. So I'd say the problem is mostly how others judge women leaders, not their actual ability.

Task 02 · Reading
  1. Sum up the plot of the 1924 movie.
  2. What could be the link of "prefers to tend to stray cats" to today's US presidential race?
  3. What tropes in the movies have made them 'acceptable' to audiences?

翻译: 1. 概括 1924 年那部电影的情节. 2. 「宁愿去照顾流浪猫」这句话和如今的美国总统大选可能有什么联系? 3. 这些电影里有哪些套路, 让 (女总统) 这件事在观众眼里变得「可以接受」?

思路: 第 1 题只说故事, 不评价, 三四句就够. 第 2 题文章本身没提大选, 要联系 2024 年竞选里的「childless cat ladies」一说. 第 3 题文章列了好几种套路 (trope), 逐条找出来, 每条用一句话说清它为什么让观众「放心」: 要么让这件事显得不真实, 要么让女总统显得不够格.

答案 (先自己做再看)

1. The Last Man on Earth is a silent comedy set in 1954. A disease called "masculitis" has killed every adult man except Elmer, who was living alone in a forest when it struck. He's brought back to civilisation, the government buys him for $10m, and two female senators box each other for the right to marry him. Elmer only wants his childhood sweetheart, though. The country is run by a woman, and the White House is a mess.

2. In the film the president would rather look after stray cats than run the country, so she comes across as a lonely cat lady who isn't up to the job. In 2024 that old cliché was back: JD Vance had called leading Democrats "childless cat ladies", and that was used against Kamala Harris, who has no children of her own. Taylor Swift even signed her endorsement of Harris "Childless Cat Lady".

3.

  • The story is set in the future or in some odd alternate world, so it doesn't feel real.
  • She doesn't win a normal election. She gets the job because of a crisis, usually after the male president dies or resigns.
  • The "good" ones don't actually want the job and have to be pushed into it. The ambitious ones turn out to be untrustworthy or end badly.
  • She's distracted from governing by her husband and kids (or by cats).
  • The early films play it for laughs, so nobody has to take the idea seriously.

易错: 第 1 题不要写成影评, 审查机构骂它「下流」之类的内容不属于情节. 第 2 题的 cat lady 联系是文章外的背景, 写的时候点明是 2024 年竞选里的说法.

Explain the following phrases in other words in English

Task 02 · Vocabulary

Explain the following phrases in other words in English:

  • save one tree-dwelling hermit
  • have to be dragged into public service
  • a lingering trope
  • to be different from the mould
  • a progressive premise

翻译: 用别的英文说法解释下面这些短语.

思路: 先回文章找到原句. 注意 save 在这里是介词 (= except), 不是「拯救」; mould 是做东西用的模子, 引申为固定的样式; premise 在电影里指故事的基本设定. 写完放回原句读一遍.

答案 (先自己做再看)
表达换个说法文中的用法
save one tree-dwelling hermitexcept for one man living alone up in the trees, away from everyone影评人的玩笑: 要让女人当选总统, 得先让地球上所有男人死光, 只剩一个住在树上的隐士, 而他大概连选民登记都没做.

Task 02 · Discussion
  1. Are there any similarities in these movies with the current presidential race?
  2. Do you think Kamala Harris's chances of becoming president are reduced due to her being female?

翻译: 1. 这些电影和当前的总统竞选有相似之处吗? 2. 你认为卡玛拉·哈里斯因为是女性, 当选总统的机会就变小了吗?

思路: 第 1 题拿文章里的套路逐条对照 2024 年的真实情况, 有像的也有不像的. 第 2 题是观点题; 文章写于 2024 年 10 月大选前, 现在已经知道结果 (哈里斯输给了特朗普), 回答时可以顺带说一句, 但重点仍是「性别到底起了多大作用」.

答案 (先自己做再看)

1. Yes, a few. Harris didn't win a primary. She only became the candidate because Biden pulled out, which is a bit like the screen women who step in after a crisis. The "cat lady" attacks and the comments about her not having children are the same old focus on a woman's family life. The big difference is that she actually had to win an ordinary election.

2. Probably a little, yes. Some voters still find it hard to picture a woman as commander in chief, and Hillary Clinton ran into the same thing in 2016. But I don't think gender was the main reason she lost to Trump. Prices, immigration, Biden's unpopularity and the very short campaign mattered more.

文章脉络 (方便复习时快速回忆):

  • 开头: 1924 年的默片 The Last Man on Earth 是最早出现女总统的电影之一, 但它把女人掌权当成笑话: 白宫杂草丛生, 总统只顾照顾流浪猫.
  • 为什么少见: 影视要赚钱, 总统一直是传统男性气质和家庭价值的象征, 女总统可能吓跑保守观众.
  • 套路一 · 未来: 故事设在未来或另类世界 (Project Moonbase、Back to the Future Part II、The Simpsons 等).
  • 套路二 · 危机: 女人不是正常选上的, 而是男总统死了或辞职后接手 (Commander in Chief、Veep、House of Cards).
  • 套路三 · 不情愿: 好的女总统都不想当; 有野心的不可信或下场悲惨. 男总统可以既有原则又有野心.
  • 套路四 · 分心: 被丈夫孩子 (或猫) 分心, 比如 Hail to the Chief、Commander in Chief.
  • 变化: 21 世纪的女总统比 20 世纪多, 性别不再总是重点 (Don't Look Up、State of Affairs); 希拉里 2016 年的竞选让这类角色更严肃. 现实和影视互相影响, 是个反馈循环.
  • 结尾: 1964 年的 Kisses for My President 前面很进步, 结尾却让女总统怀孕辞职, 回到「女人不能既工作又带孩子」.

Discussing a Sample CV源文件 Career_Graduate Trainee_Peter Hunzicker_CV.pdf;内容: 小组讨论一份英文样例简历.

Differences and unusual elements of the sample CV

Task 03 · Group discussion

In your group discuss the differences/unusual elements of the sample CV concerning:

  1. Layout
  2. Personal information
  3. Education
  4. Work experience
  5. Other elements?
Sample CV · Page 1–2

翻译: 小组讨论这份样例简历在以下方面和你熟悉的简历有什么不同、有什么不寻常的地方: 1. 版式; 2. 个人信息; 3. 教育; 4. 工作经历; 5. 其他?

思路: 拿它和德国的 Lebenslauf 比最容易看出门道. 这是一个德国学生写给英语国家雇主的简历, 很多地方是为了让英国读者看懂、或者照英美习惯改过的. 每一项找两三点, 说清「是什么」和「为什么这样写」就够了.

答案 (先自己做再看)

1. Layout

  • Newest first: the Master's comes before the Bachelor's.
  • It opens with an objective and a short list of key skills, so you know what he wants before you read anything else.
  • Dates on the left, details on the right, clear headings. Easy to skim.
  • No photo, no signature, no place and date at the end.
  • Two pages, but page 2 is almost empty. It could fit on one.

2. Personal information

  • Only name, address, email and phone.
  • No date of birth, nationality, marital status or photo. That's normal in the UK and US, where employers don't want anything that could lead to discrimination. A German CV often has all of it.

3. Education

  • German grades come with British equivalents (1.8 is "a very good upper second"), and the Abitur is compared to A-levels. A British reader wouldn't know these otherwise.
  • He gives an expected grade and graduation date for a degree he hasn't finished yet.
  • Nothing before the Abitur. No primary school.

4. Work experience

  • It's called "Experience", not "Work experience", so unpaid things count too: a student project and community service.
  • Each job has bullet points that start with a verb and show results: "two successful product introductions", "the location's first ... system".
  • "Community service instead of military service" is explained, because Zivildienst means nothing to someone abroad.

5. Other elements

  • Language level is given with CEFR (C1), not just "good English".
  • The interests say something about him: mentoring first-generation students, competitive swimming.
  • "References available upon request" is a typical British line. Many people now leave it out because it's taken for granted.
  • A few weak spots: "strong organisational and interpersonal skills" is a cliché with no proof, he's "manager" of Uni-Face in one place and "director" in another, and Adobe Flash has been dead since 2020.

Task 04
Does an Oxbridge Degree Make a Difference?阅读材料: Does an Oxbridge degree make a difference?, BBC News, 2016 年 9 月 2 日.

阅读讨论
Read the article and discuss

Task 04 · Reading
  1. What were the expectations of state school pupils before going to Oxbridge?
  2. What did they find was the reality when they studied there?
  3. Do they think that studying there has made a difference to their careers? Why? Why not?

翻译: a. 公立学校的学生去牛剑之前有什么预期? b. 真正在那里读书时, 他们发现实际情况是怎样的? c. 他们觉得在那里读书对自己的职业有影响吗? 为什么 / 为什么没有?

思路: 文章里有两个公立学校出身的人: Freya Pryce (威尔士 Aberystwyth 的综合中学, 2011 年进牛津) 和校长 Ian Bauckham (当年读剑桥). 三道题都按「Freya 怎么说, Bauckham 怎么说」来答, 每题两三句, 尽量用文中的细节. 注意 Bauckham 没说他入学前怎么想, a 题主要靠 Freya, 再加上牛津发言人的话.

答案 (先自己做再看)

a. Mostly negative. Freya thought everyone would be "really nerdy and posh" and that Oxford was elitist, so she didn't want to apply at first. An Oxford spokeswoman says a lot of state school sixth-formers still feel "Oxford isn't the kind of place for me".

b. Partly what they feared. Freya says the students still weren't a normal mix of society, and she was shocked that "rich boys' clubs" still exist. But she also met friendly, open-minded people from all kinds of backgrounds. The work was hard (up to three essays a week) and she lost a lot of sleep in her first year. Bauckham found it tough at the start because he'd never been around people with so much confidence and social advantage.

c. Yes, both say it helped. Freya's skills and Oxford's name helped her get an internship and research work in international development. Bauckham says Cambridge helped him move up in teaching, and that he's never met anyone who regrets going. Freya isn't sure it's all down to the teaching, though. You can learn the same skills at other universities, so she thinks part of it is just status.

文中数字 (2016 年的数据):

  • 公立学校学生占比: 牛津近 60% (40 年来最高), 剑桥 62%. 但全国 93% 的学生在公立学校.
  • 申请人数: 牛津 19,500 人争 3,500 个名额, 剑桥 16,719 人争约 3,400 个名额.
  • Bauckham 的担忧: 录取很看面试, 而面试需要自信和临场应变, 精英私校的学生从小在家里和朋友圈就耳濡目染, 综合中学的学生吃亏.

  • Biomedical Sciences: 'Why do cat's eyes appear to 'glow' in the dark?'

翻译: a. 哪些建议你觉得有用 / 没用? 为什么? b. 他们在考生身上找什么样的能力? c. 小组里试着给文中列出的面试题答出有说服力的回答.

思路: 文章分两块: 面试前 (别去搜面试官、别想什么都学、回到基础、专攻一两个感兴趣的话题) 和面试时 (把想法说出来、先要时间思考、不会就承认、被反驳时怎么办、最后别乱提问). a 题挑两三条有用的、一两条有保留的, 各说一句理由. b 题从这些建议反推. c 题重点不是答案对不对, 而是像文章说的那样「带着面试官走一遍你的思路」, 所以答案里要有推理过程, 最好也承认另一面.

答案 (先自己做再看)

a.

  • Useful: thinking out loud. The interviewer can't help you or give you credit if you sit there in silence, and it takes the pressure off getting the "right" answer.
  • Useful: asking for 30 seconds to think. Most people wouldn't dare to, so it's good to know it's allowed.
  • Useful: rereading your personal statement, and admitting when you're wrong instead of defending a lost point.
  • Less useful or a bit odd: "don't ask any questions at the end". In most job interviews you're told the opposite, so it feels risky. Also, "don't try to learn everything" sounds relaxing, but you still need very solid basics, so it's easy to take too far.
  • One thing to keep in mind: Dr Agarwal runs UniAdmissions, a company that sells interview coaching, so he isn't completely neutral.

b. They aren't really testing knowledge. They want to see how you think: whether you can use what you know on a new problem, reason step by step and explain it clearly. They also look for real interest in the subject (reading beyond school, not just "I want to be a doctor"), honesty about what you don't know, and flexibility, so you can take on a counter-argument or fix your own mistake. In short, someone who would do well in a tutorial.

c.

  • Biology: In many ways the sea is easier. Water holds you up, so you don't need a strong skeleton, you can't dry out, and the temperature hardly changes. Reproduction is easier too, since many animals just release eggs and sperm into the water. But there's much less oxygen in water, and light only reaches the top layer. On land you have to fight gravity and dehydration. Life started in the sea and moved onto land quite late, which suggests land is the harder place.
  • Physics: While the force is acting, the ball speeds up at a constant rate, so the graph is a straight line going up. (That assumes the force is bigger than its weight.) When the force stops, only gravity acts, so the velocity falls in a straight line with slope −g. It reaches zero at the highest point, becomes negative as the ball falls, and keeps getting more negative until the ball hits the ground. I'm ignoring air resistance; with it, the falling part would curve and level off.
  • Psychology: One idea is that first-borns get their parents' full attention for a while, and later the attention is shared. Another is that older children learn by teaching their younger siblings. But I'd first check the study. If it compares different families, big families have more younger siblings and might differ in other ways, like income. A within-family comparison would be fairer. Also, the effect is real but quite small.
  • Modern Languages: Most people in the world still don't speak English well, and French is spoken on five continents, with the number of speakers growing fast in Africa. A language also gives you access to a culture, its books, films and humour, in a way translation can't. If you only speak English, people always have to meet you on your terms. Learning French means you can meet them on theirs. Machine translation helps, but it doesn't replace that.
  • Biomedical Sciences: They don't actually make light. Cats have a reflective layer behind the retina called the tapetum lucidum. Light that passes through the retina without being absorbed gets bounced back, so the light-sensitive cells get a second chance to catch it. That helps cats see in very dim light. If you shine a torch or headlights at them, some of that reflected light comes back out of the eye, which is why it looks like a glow.

易错: c 题别只报结论. 物理题的图要说清两段直线的斜率、最高点速度为零、下落时速度为负; 心理学题要想到「研究本身可能有问题」, 这正是面试官想听的.
